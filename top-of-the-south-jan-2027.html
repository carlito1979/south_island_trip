How to read the water marks

Proper swimming

A pool at the park, or a beach that is genuinely safe to swim at. Marked in blue on every stop.

Limited

Hot tub, thermal pool, paddling, or swimming that depends on the day's conditions.

Look, don't swim

Rips, driftwood, cold open coast. Beautiful, but not for an eight-year-old.

Sea temperatures are approximate January regional averages and could be 2°C either side. None of these beaches are patrolled — treat every swim as unsupervised and judge conditions on the day.

The route, night by night

Christchurch Airport → Hanmer Springs · 135 km · 1h45 Van collected 2pm. Straight up SH1 then inland at Waipara.
012 nights

Sat 9 – Sun 10 January

Hanmer Springs

Hanmer Springs TOP 10 Holiday Park · 5 Hanmer Springs Road

Water here

No pool at the park, but private wood-fired hot tubs at about $39 an hour. The Thermal Pools are a 400 m walk: hot pools at varying temperatures, waterslides, a lazy river and a kids' play area. Outside food is allowed in.

  • Sat 9Collect the van at 2pm, drive up, settle in. Book a hot tub for the first evening — it's the cheapest good thing on the whole trip.
  • Sun 10Thermal Pools for the morning, before the queues build. Afternoon: A-Maze-N-Golf and the maze are next door, the Animal Park is a five-minute walk, and the Dog Stream Waterfall track runs from the forest behind the park.
  • OptionalJet boating and bungy at Ferry Bridge with Thrillseekers, 10 minutes back down the valley.

On site  Jumping pillow, trampoline, playground, pedal karts and mountain bike hire. The park is walkable to the village, so leave the van parked for two days.

Hanmer Springs → Kaikōura · 130 km · 1h45 Take the Inland Road via Waiau, not back down to SH1. Same time, better scenery, no coastal roadworks.
022 nights

Mon 11 – Tue 12 January

Kaikōura

Kaikōura TOP 10 Holiday Park · Beach Road, Kaikōura

Water here

Swimming pool at the park — this is where you swim. The local beaches are gravel and exposed, and the sea sits near 15°C. Short walk to the Whale Watch departure point.

  • Mon 11Point Kean seal colony at the end of the peninsula. Free, and in January there are pups in the shallow rock pools. Follow with the Kaikōura Peninsula Walkway along the clifftop, and crayfish or fish and chips in town.
  • Tue 12Whale Watch, 7:15am check-in. Roughly 3.5 hours by catamaran. Take the earliest sailing — calmest seas, and the 10:00am and 12:45pm sailings remain as fallbacks if yours is cancelled for weather.
  • Tue 12Afternoon at the pool, or the Fyffe House colonial cottage in town if energy is low.

Watch for  Seasickness — medicate one to two hours before departure, not once symptoms start. Sandflies at Point Kean. Stay 20 metres from seals and never put yourself between a seal and the water.

Kaikōura → Motueka · 370 km · 4h45 · longest leg Break it at Pelorus Bridge around midday for lunch and a river swim. The car park fills by 10:30 on a hot day, so a late arrival is fine.
033 nights

Wed 13 – Fri 15 January

Motueka

Motueka TOP 10 Holiday Park · Motueka

Water here — the best of the trip

Pool at the park, plus a private spa included free with TOP 10 membership. Kaiteriteri is 20 minutes away: golden sand, calm water, a tidal lagoon, and a sea temperature near 19°C. This is the warmest, safest swimming you'll find on the whole loop.

  • Wed 13Arrive late afternoon after the long drive. Pool, then the free loaner bikes around Motueka.
  • Thu 14Abel Tasman water taxi. Wilsons, departing Kaiteriteri 10:30 and returning 4:15, to Anchorage Beach — 45 minutes each way. Five and a half hours of golden sand, plus the optional Pitt Head loop to Te Pukatea if she's got the legs.
  • Fri 15Winery lunch in Upper Moutere (three options overleaf, decision open). Afternoon at Kaiteriteri or back at the camp pool.

Watch for  The Kaiteriteri car park is full by 10am in January. Go early or park at Little Kaiteriteri and walk around. Split Apple Rock is a short walk from Towers Bay if you're not taking the boat.

Motueka → Carters Beach · 245 km · 3h15 Over the Hope Saddle and down the Buller Gorge. Stop at the swingbridge — longest in New Zealand — and the Comet Line zipline if she wants it.
042 nights

Sat 16 – Sun 17 January

Carters Beach, Westport

Carters Beach TOP 10 Holiday Park · 57 Marine Parade

Water here

No pool, but a safe sandy swimming beach 100 metres from the front gate — the only genuinely swimmable West Coast beach on your route. Hot tubs for hire on site. Sea around 16°C, so a rashie helps.

  • Sat 16Arrive mid-afternoon. Beach, jumping pillow, and the games room with air hockey and foosball.
  • Sun 17Cape Foulwind seal colony, 15 minutes away and free. Walk the clifftop track through to Tauranga Bay and back — about 90 minutes with stops. Afternoon back at the beach.
  • OptionalThe Kawatiri Coastal Trail starts outside the gate. Denniston Incline is 30 minutes north for the mining history if the weather turns.

Ask at booking  Request a hardstanding site — the grass turns to mud in West Coast rain. Reception closes at 7pm in summer. Sandfly repellent goes on before you open the van door.

Carters Beach → Greymouth · 100 km · 1h30 The Great Coast Road. Only 90 minutes of driving, so the day belongs to Punakaiki and Shantytown.
052 nights

Mon 18 – Tue 19 January

Greymouth

Greymouth Seaside TOP 10 Holiday Park · Karoro, Greymouth

Water here — the one dry stop

No pool, and the beach is not swimmable: rips and driftwood. There is a private spa pool for hire on site. The Grey District Aquatic Centre is about ten minutes away and covers the gap if you need a swim day.

  • Mon 18Pancake Rocks at Punakaiki on the way through — a 20-minute loop walk. Time your arrival near high tide or you get rocks without the blowholes. Parking is free for 20 minutes, then $5 an hour.
  • Mon 18Shantytown Heritage Park, open 10am to 3pm. Working steam train, sawmill, sluice gun and gold panning at roughly $7–10 a pan. The best-fitting activity on the entire route: trains, machines and making things in one place. Build the visit around the train departure times.
  • Tue 19Hokitika Gorge — 2.4 km easy loop with two swing bridges. The turquoise only shows after dry weather. Then a pounamu or bone carving session in Hokitika, and the glow worm dell in town at dusk, which is free.

On site  Mini golf, go-karts, playground and a games room. Sandflies are bad at the gorge — repellent on before you leave the van.

Greymouth → Akaroa · 330 km · 4h45 Over Arthur's Pass and across the Canterbury Plains, then the Summit Road into the crater. Lunch at Arthur's Pass village and the Devils Punchbowl Falls walk if legs allow.
063 nights

Wed 20 – Fri 22 January

Akaroa

Akaroa TOP 10 Holiday Park · Akaroa

Water here

Decent-sized pool at the park. The main beach in Akaroa is sheltered harbour water and safe for swimming, at around 18°C. Ōkains Bay, 35 minutes over the hill, has proper sand and surf if you want a beach day.

  • Wed 20Arrive late afternoon after the long drive. Settle in, pool, and walk down into town for dinner.
  • Thu 21Black Cat Hector's dolphin swim. Maximum 12 swimmers, minimum age 8, sheltered harbour, 5mm wetsuits and hot showers afterwards. Follow with the Giant's House mosaic garden — a whole hillside of broken-china sculpture, and the closest thing to an art lesson she'll agree to.
  • Fri 22Deliberately kept clear. If the dolphin swim is cancelled for conditions on Wednesday, this is your second attempt. Otherwise: Ōkains Bay, the harbour cruise, or nothing at all.

Watch for  Kea at Arthur's Pass will strip the rubber seals off the van — don't leave it unattended with food visible. Ask for a hardstanding site at Akaroa for the harbour view. The walking track into town is steep coming back up, so don't over-shop.

Akaroa → Christchurch Airport · 100 km · 1h45 Leave by 07:30. Refuel at Little River or Lincoln, where it's cheaper than Akaroa. Van back by 10am.

Sat 23 January

Home

The Summit Road is not a route to rush, and JUCY will want the van refuelled and reasonably clean. Allow 45 minutes at the depot before the 10am deadline. Flight back to Auckland later that day.

Winery lunch, Friday 15 January — your call

All three are in Upper Moutere, 15 to 25 minutes from the Motueka park. Note that Forsters no longer exists — the owners sold the restaurant, so any guide recommending it is out of date.

Confirmed trading

Moutere Hills Restaurant & Cellar Door

A sit-down restaurant lunch with the cellar door tasting at the table, on a deck looking over the vines and neighbouring hop gardens. The most polished meal of the three, and the most formal for an eight-year-old. Choose this if the lunch itself is the point.

42 Eggers Road · 03 543 2288 · rated 4.7–4.8
mouterehillsrestaurantandcellardoor.co.nz

Ring first

Kahurangi Estate

Land or sea platters around $30 in a sheltered, sunny courtyard off the tasting room. Home to the oldest Riesling vines in the South Island. But the regional winegrowers now point visitors to Heaphy Vineyards instead, which suggests the cellar door may have stopped hosting. Heaphy's café does woodfired pizza, which solves a child's lunch instantly.

Upper Moutere · verify by phone before driving out
tastenelsonwines.nz/kahurangi-estate

Confirmed trading

Neudorf Vineyards

A shared picnic basket at a booked table in the wine garden. Room to roam, dogs and children explicitly welcome, and the most acclaimed wine of the three — organic, dry-farmed Moutere Chardonnay and Pinot Noir. The pick if you'd rather not spend an hour asking her to sit still.

138 Neudorf Road · 03 543 2643 · tastings ~$15pp
neudorf.co.nz/pages/visit
